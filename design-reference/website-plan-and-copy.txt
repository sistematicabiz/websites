Avoid duplicating everything. The consulting site can introduce you briefly and link to the personal site for “writing, teaching, and public work.” The personal site can mention Sistem(at)ica as your independent advisory practice and link out for services.

This separation lets each site remain disciplined. It also protects the personal site from becoming a résumé and the consulting site from becoming a long intellectual biography.

The “why Sistem(at)ica” section

Why Sistem(at)ica

Sistem(at)ica brings together two commitments.

The first is systematic: disciplined research, analytical clarity, intellectual honesty, and attention to evidence. Complex questions deserve more than fashionable language or easy conclusions.

The second is systems thinking: an understanding that financial, social, ecological, and political outcomes are interconnected. The most consequential challenges cannot be understood—or addressed—one variable at a time.

The name also carries an Italian flair. Sistematica evokes both method and sensibility: rigor without reductionism; coherence without rigidity; analysis attentive to the wider whole.

This is the approach behind the work: rigorous enough for decision-making, expansive enough for the realities clients are trying to navigate.

Land, learning & regeneration

Alongside my work in finance, history, theology and the economy, I am deepening my understanding of regenerative farming and the relationships among land, food, community, and economic life. My work and learning at Princeton Theological Seminary’s Farminary (https://ptsem.edu/academics/departments/farminary/) bring these questions into a setting where theological reflection meets the practical work of growing food and caring for soil.

I am interested not only in how we cultivate land, but in what working with it can teach us about interdependence, limits, care, and responsibility—especially those of us whose lives as economists, city dwellers, white-collar professionals, or academics can keep us at a distance from the living systems on which we depend. This is an ongoing process of learning, one that invites me to reconsider the assumptions about productivity, value, and prosperity that shape my work elsewhere.

Oikos Lab

An idea in development

These interests are also informing Oikos Lab, an idea I am developing to connect land-based learning, ecological care, and new approaches to economic life.

I am exploring how universities and other learning communities might bring food growing, ecological restoration, and reflection on economics and ethics into closer relationship, using their land not simply as a backdrop to education, but as a place of learning, nourishment, and shared responsibility.

The aim is to connect ideas with practice: to ask what institutions oriented toward ecological resilience and the common good might look like, and how we might begin to build them.

Design direction

The visual goal should be intellectual confidence, warmth, and restraint.

Sistem(at)ica

-   Palette: warm ivory or paper white; charcoal or deep navy typography; one understated accent such as olive, terracotta, muted rust, or mineral green.

-   Typography: a serious serif for headlines paired with a clean sans serif for body text. This can quietly communicate rigor plus humanistic range.

-   Visual language: subtle grids, diagrams, data textures, topographic or relational-line motifs—not generic stock images of skyscrapers, handshakes, or “green finance” leaves.

-   Photography: one strong, approachable professional portrait of you; otherwise use abstract or documentary-style visual material sparingly.

-   Logo usage: retain the parenthesis in “Sistem(at)ica.” It visually expresses the conceptual pairing: systematic and systems.

LauraSegafredo.com

-   More editorial and spacious than the business site.

-   Let essays, episode titles, images from fieldwork/travel where appropriate, and thoughtful portraiture carry the site.

-   Use a shared typographic family or a subtle shared color accent to signal that the sites belong to the same person, but give the personal site more white space and a more magazine-like rhythm.

-   Make every external project visibly labeled. Someone arriving from Google should never wonder where to find the podcast, fund, think tank, or faculty role.
